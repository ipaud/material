## Three improvements that would most raise competitiveness

1. **Per-layer activity windows + keyframes in one render (#6).** The workflow
   in the report took 7 passes, ~1 h of render and ~30 GB of generational loss;
   Resolume/TouchDesigner/AE do this in one timeline.
2. **Headless CLI on the deterministic export (#5).** It makes MATERIAL
   scriptable and CI-testable, which none of the live tools do cleanly.
3. **Hardware-encoded, async-readback export (#10)** — real-time-ish
   rendering is what separates an instrument from a batch tool; the ~8 fps
   figure is the biggest practical gap versus After Effects/Notch.

## Video ingest (post-0.8.4, uncommitted)

`src/rendering/layers/VideoIngest.ts`: a video source is copied through a
built-in material (which decodes) into an SRGB8 target; `LayerRuntimeImpl.techniqueSource()`
hands that copy to technique shaders. Optical-flow analysis and the temporal
ring still read the raw video (their numbers are unchanged). Measured with
`npm run gate:identity` (flat grey as video vs PNG, headless CLI): flow-displace,
motion-rgb, directional-smear 184 → 124; flow-feedback/mosh 182 → 122;
mosaic 140 → 93 and trails 142 → 93 now equal their PNG results (those two
darken by default; not a colour defect). Cost: one fullscreen copy per video
layer per frame — not profiled. Preview shares the path, so the preview of
video layers changes too.

## Export speed (#10): measured, deliberately NOT changed

Measured with a throwaway per-stage timer through the headless CLI (1080p,
H.264, 100 frames; the timer and every optimisation were discarded, so none of
this is in the code):

| Case | prepare | video seek | render+readback | IPC | fps |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| motion-rgb ×1 | 0.2 ms | 34 ms | 34 ms | 8 ms | ~13 |
| motion-rgb ×4 | 0.2 ms | 34 ms | 35 ms | 7 ms | ~13 |
| echo ×1 | 39 ms | 28 ms | 35 ms | 7 ms | ~9 |
| echo ×4 | 155 ms | 33 ms | 40 ms | 8 ms | ~4 |

- **The encoder is not the bottleneck.** Standalone 1080p ProRes: `prores_ks`
  (software) 148 fps, `prores_videotoolbox` 27× real time, `libx264` 10×. The
  render runs at 4–13 fps, so VideoToolbox would not speed the export up.
- The time is the per-frame video seek (~34 ms: decode plus waiting for a
  presented frame), the render/readback stall (~35 ms), and, for a temporal
  technique, ~39 ms per temporal layer (it seeks again).
- Tried and discarded: asynchronous pixel-buffer readback, a 2-deep ordered IPC
  pipeline, vertical flip moved into ffmpeg. The frames were bit-identical to
  the committed code for the echo project, but see the next point.
- **Finding, pre-existing and unfixed:** the export of a video source through
  `motion-rgb` (optical flow) is not run-to-run deterministic. About 1 run in 4
  (3 of 12 on the committed code) frame 2 of the export comes out as a second,
  different image; every other frame is stable. Not caused by the optimisations
  above (the committed code does it at the same rate). Hypothesis, unconfirmed:
  `waitForPresentedFrame` gives up after 60 ms and a cold 1080p seek can take
  longer, so the export draws the previous frame. Raising that timeout was
  being tested when the work was stopped. Worth fixing before trusting a
  frame-exact comparison of two exports of a flow-based video.
- Not done: #11 (history budget), #12 (FCPXML/EDL export), #13 (presets).
